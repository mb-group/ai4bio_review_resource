Five things to remember

  1. Foundation models compress data into reusable embeddings; agentic systems compose existing tools into workflows. The two are starting to meet.
  2. That meeting needs a shared language: the knowledge boundary — the inputs, tasks, and conditions where a model's output can be trusted.
  3. Nearly every model can be described by five design elements (data, architecture, training scheme, evaluation scheme, post-analysis), and each sets part of its boundary.
  4. Evidence shows real learned biology, most clearly in proteins — but independent studies repeatedly find simple baselines competitive. The boundary has four layers: missing, hard to learn, hidden by evaluation, or inaccessible.
  5. Proposal: release every model with a structured boundary statement, organized by the five design elements, and keep a community registry so researchers and agents know when to call it.
